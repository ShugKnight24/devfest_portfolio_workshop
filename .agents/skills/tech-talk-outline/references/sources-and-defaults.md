# Sources and defaults

Where every number, threshold and named method in this skill comes from, and which ones are this skill's own convention. Read it before quoting a figure to a speaker, an organizer or a report - a house default presented as research is a credibility risk for whoever repeats it.

## How to read the status column

| Status        | Meaning                                                                                        |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Measured      | Comes from a study or a platform's own instrumentation, with a sample size                     |
| Named method  | A published framework with a named author; the method is real, its effect size is not measured |
| Convention    | Repeated by experienced practitioners, never measured; treat as a strong prior                 |
| House default | Set by this skill because the task needs a number; argue with it freely                        |

## Measured findings

| Figure                        | Value                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Source                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Engagement vs. video length   | The paper's own statement: median engagement time is at most 6 minutes regardless of total video length, and viewers often make it less than halfway through videos longer than 9 minutes. Brame's summary renders this as ~100% watched under 6 min, ~50% at 9-12 min, ~20% at 12-40 min                                                          | Guo et al., 6.9M edX viewing sessions; summarized in Brame, "Effective Educational Videos", _CBE Life Sciences Education_, 2016 |
| Narration rate and engagement | Engagement usually rose with speaking rate - up to 2x within a length range - across a corpus spanning 48-254 wpm (mean 156). The "185-254 wpm band" quoted in secondary summaries is the corpus's fastest quintile, not a target the paper sets; the authors call rate a surface feature of enthusiasm and advise against forcing faster delivery | Same study                                                                                                                      |
| Intro retention window        | Percentage still watching **after the first 30 seconds** is a named metric in the platform's own retention report, alongside dips, spikes and top moments                                                                                                                                                                                          | YouTube audience-retention documentation                                                                                        |
| Intro length tolerance        | An introduction longer than roughly 5 seconds already reads as long - the counter runs on ceremony, not on substance                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Nielsen Norman Group video usability findings                                                                                   |
| Curse of knowledge            | Tappers of a well-known song predicted listeners would name it 50% of the time; listeners scored 2.5%                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Newton, Stanford, 1990, as reported in Heath & Heath, _Made to Stick_ - cite it as their retelling                              |

Two cautions apply before reusing these figures:

- The video figures come from instructional video and platform analytics, not from conference rooms - a seated conference audience cannot skip, so the length cliff constrains the talk's **recording**, not the talk itself.
- A retention curve locates a failure without explaining it - rewatch the segment before rewriting it.

Names and attributions for the Bow and Arrow, the Accordion and the 5-Second Moment are reliable. The wording used here is paraphrase, so credit the method without quoting it as the author's own sentence.

## Practitioner conventions

Widely repeated, never measured:

- Terminal text at 24pt or larger, editor text at 20pt or larger (speaking.io, and repeated across developer-advocacy speaking guidance).
- Slide count is not a rule - Holman runs ~100 slides in 30 minutes and also holds one for five minutes.
- 10-3-1 for a demo: rehearse 10 times, hold 3 checkpoints, keep 1 recorded backup.
- Holman on live demos, verbatim: _"Live demos are like Global Thermonuclear War: the only way to win is to not do a live demo in front of hundreds of strangers in the first place."_
- Holman on outlines, verbatim: _"I just use an outline as a point to channel my thoughts, not as the verbatim bible of my talk."_
- Slot archetypes (lightning 5-10 min, session 25-45, keynote 45-60) come from conference-programme convention, not from a standard.

## House defaults (this skill's own)

None of these is a published threshold. Each exists because the outline needs a decision, and each is meant to be argued with once the speaker has one timed run:

| Default                 | Value                                                                                                                                                                                       | Why this value                                                                                                |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unused buffer           | 10-15% of the slot                                                                                                                                                                          | Absorbs laughter, A/V handover and one long question - the three overruns rehearsal never shows               |
| Mode change             | At least every 7 minutes                                                                                                                                                                    | Long enough to land a pillar, short enough that a drifting listener gets a new entry point                    |
| Demo budget             | 2x its rehearsed length                                                                                                                                                                     | Stage typing, narration and audience reaction all stretch it                                                  |
| Speaking after the demo | At least 2 minutes                                                                                                                                                                          | Keeps an overrun from eating the conclusion                                                                   |
| Pillars                 | 2-3                                                                                                                                                                                         | Three sections is the inherited default; a fourth pillar reliably steals the buffer                           |
| Dead ends in arc 1      | At most a third of the body                                                                                                                                                                 | Beyond that the insight arrives with no room for proof                                                        |
| Quality gate            | Two consecutive timed runs inside slot-minus-buffer, one-idea and title-as-takeaway per slide, evidence per pillar, demo ending 2 minutes early, a colleague restating the arrow unprompted | Assembled from the accordion method and the per-slide test, and set by this skill rather than by any standard |

Post-talk signals (feedback score, hallway and social repetition of the arrow, retention on the published recording, repo traffic in the following 48 hours) are **directional only**. Report them as movement against the speaker's own previous talk, and refuse to set a target number for them.
